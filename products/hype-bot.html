THE EDGE

The signal is ordinary.
The patience is not.

The signal behind the Hype Bot is a simple momentum read, the kind every trader has seen, and on any single chart a signal like that is close to worthless — it fires constantly and most fires are noise. What changes its character is refusing to act on anything else: only completed bars, no averaging down, no second position, and no override when the chart looks tempting. The exact settings are private; every trade is published.

HOW IT EXITS

The exit is where the work went.

Every position carries two exits. A 10% hard stop, resting on the exchange, is the floor. Above it sits a tight trailing take-profit — once the trade is in profit it follows the best price reached and never moves backwards.

The detail that makes it work is when and how the trail is allowed to take over. That timing is the most tested part of the strategy, and it stays private.

Why the exit is the strategy

A trail that takes over too eagerly gets stopped out on the first wobble — booking a rounding error and, after fees, often a small loss. Tested across the full HYPE history at one-minute resolution, getting that one detail wrong turns the strategy from clearly profitable into a net loser. Same entries, same hard stop. The only difference is the exit.

HONEST LIMITS

What the Hype Bot isn't

Three sources, labelled

The record has three parts. Before 6 November 2025 it is the TradingView strategy tester, which models the trailing stop at bar resolution rather than tick by tick. From 6 November 2025 it is every signal as published to subscribers, at the price each signal carried. From 23 September 2026 it is the engine's own fills on its Bybit HYPE account. All three are charged the same 0.12% round trip: we reconciled 96 live signals against the fills and fees on our own Hyperliquid account, and 0.12% was the real cost, with 96% of signals reaching the account. Only the last part is realised fills, so treat the earlier figures as a model, and replay them at your own costs.

One asset, in both directions

HYPE has not only gone up. It fell 66% from its high of $59.47 on 18 September 2025 to $20.46 on 21 January 2026, and the trades opened in that window made +53.1% at 1x (54 trades, 76% winners; the first 20 from the strategy tester, the last 34 live published signals). Because it trades short as well as long, it does not need HYPE to rise. What it cannot do is diversify: when HYPE is quiet or choppy, the strategy has nothing else to trade.

High win rate, poor payoff

A tight trail wins often and small, and loses rarely but large — the average loss is several times the average win. That shape depends entirely on the win rate holding. If it slips a few points, expectancy inverts quickly. A high win rate is not the same thing as a safe strategy.

Leverage means liquidation risk

The hard stop sits well inside the liquidation buffer at the operating leverage, and it rests on the exchange so it fires whether the bot is running or not. It is not a guarantee: a gap through the level fills below it. Do not commit capital you cannot afford to lose.
